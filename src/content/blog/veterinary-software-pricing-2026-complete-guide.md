Veterinary practice management software (PIMS) is a multi-year commitment — and the monthly price you see on a vendor's website is rarely the price you pay. Between per-user fees, implementation charges, integration costs, and surprise upgrade fees, the real cost of veterinary software is often double the advertised rate by the end of year one.

In this guide, we break down the published 2026 pricing of the four systems most commonly quoted to clinics — ezyVet, AVImark, Provet Cloud, and VETEK — then walk through the hidden costs that make up total cost of ownership (TCO). We cover annual discounts worth negotiating and close with a set of questions to ask any vendor before you sign. If you are still comparing features rather than prices, read our [guide to choosing veterinary practice management software](/blog/how-to-choose-veterinary-practice-management-software/) first.

## Veterinary Software Pricing in 2026: The Complete Breakdown

In 2026, veterinary practice management software costs between $30 and $600 per month, depending on the vendor, your clinic size, and the modules you add. The published ranges are ezyVet at $245-$600, AVImark at $150-$400, Provet Cloud at $99 per veterinarian plus a $249 platform fee, and VETEK at $30-$99 per clinic, all-inclusive.

| Vendor | Published monthly price (2026) | Pricing model | Best suited for |
|--------|-------------------------------|---------------|-----------------|
| ezyVet | $245 - $600 | Per clinic, module tiers | Specialists, referral hospitals, large multi-branch groups |
| AVImark | $150 - $400 | Per location, on-premise license | Single-location clinics wanting offline, on-site systems |
| Provet Cloud | $99/vet + $249 platform | Per veterinarian + platform fee | Teams that accept headcount-based billing |
| VETEK | $30 - $99 | All-inclusive, per clinic | Multi-branch practices wanting one flat, predictable fee |

Why are the ranges so wide? Vendor websites list "starting at" prices for a minimal configuration, and the actual figure depends on three things: how many modules you enable (inventory, clinical records, point of sale, imaging), how many users or locations you have, and what you negotiate. Before comparing quotes, write down your real requirements — branches, staff accounts, modules — so you compare comparable configurations. The American Veterinary Medical Association's [practice management resources](https://www.avma.org) provide a useful neutral reference for what a modern PIMS should include before you evaluate cost.

One rule saves clinics more than any negotiation tactic: compare written quotes, not sales calls. A quote that itemizes modules, users, implementation, and annual-increase terms is a document you can evaluate; a call is a performance. Ask every vendor for the same itemized format, and the comparison takes minutes instead of weeks.

## ezyVet Pricing: $245-$600/Month

ezyVet is priced per clinic, with the final figure depending on the modules you enable: core practice management starts near $245 per month, while adding imaging, telemedicine, and advanced analytics pushes monthly costs toward $600 for larger practices. Add-ons such as integrated payments and lab connectivity are billed on top of the base.

What drives the price up:

- **Module selection** — each module (clinical, imaging, appointment management) adds to the base fee
- **Integration add-ons** — lab integrations, payment processing, and telemedicine are separate line items
- **Implementation and training** — typically invoiced as an upfront project fee, often equal to several months of subscription
- **Annual increases** — most subscription contracts allow price adjustments at renewal, so the year-one quote is not the year-five bill

ezyVet makes the most sense for specialty and referral hospitals that will genuinely use the full breadth of modules. For a general practice or a smaller multi-branch group, the top of the range is easy to reach once add-ons are stacked — and the features that cost extra are often exactly the ones a clinic needs. The [AAHA](https://www.aaha.org) standards for veterinary practice give a solid baseline of what a well-equipped clinic actually needs versus what is simply nice to have.

## AVImark Pricing: $150-$400/Month

AVImark is an on-premise system sold by license: published 2026 monthly costs range from about $150 to $400, depending on the modules selected and the number of workstations. Because it runs on your own hardware, you pay separately for servers, installation, backup infrastructure, and ongoing maintenance — costs many clinics underestimate.

The real cost picture for AVImark:

- **Hardware** — server, workstations, network, and backup storage are your responsibility
- **Installation and conversion** — migrating from paper or a previous system is typically billed per hour
- **Maintenance and support** — ongoing updates and support contracts add to the license fee
- **IT staff time** — someone in your practice owns backups, updates, and troubleshooting, or you pay a contractor

AVImark suits single-location clinics with strong on-site IT and a genuine offline-first requirement. But the on-premise model has a second cost few budget for: every branch you open means another installation, another server, and another maintenance contract. For multi-location groups, a cloud system removes that infrastructure spend entirely — one reason the [VHMA](https://www.vhma.org) benchmarks show cloud adoption continuing to climb through 2026.

The per-vet model has a subtle effect beyond cost: it discourages growth, because every new hire increases your software bill. The platform fee covers the base system, while imaging (PACS), SMS messaging, and advanced reporting are typically sold as add-ons. For a clinic that adds a veterinarian every year or two, the cumulative cost difference against a flat-fee system is significant — a five-vet clinic pays roughly $700 more per month for the same core feature set, every month, forever.

VETEK is priced flat per clinic at $30-$99 per month with every module included: inventory with batch tracking, clinical records, point of sale, and multi-branch management. There are no per-user fees, so a two-vet practice and a ten-vet practice pay the same base price for the same feature set.

Annual billing is available at a typical 10-20% discount over monthly billing, which brings a $99 monthly plan to roughly $79-89 per month billed yearly.

This is the difference between transparent and quote-based pricing: VETEK's price list, module list, and fee structure are published and verifiable before a single sales call, while quote-based vendors gate the same information behind demos and negotiation. Transparency is not just a budget convenience — it is a signal of how the vendor will treat you after the signature. A vendor that cannot publish what the system costs will also be vague about what the system does.

Every vendor on this list offers a trial or demo — insist on testing with your own workflows before committing, and ask exactly what is excluded from the quoted price. A genuine evaluation period is the only way to verify that the modules you are paying for work the way the demo showed: import a real product list, run a sale that deducts stock, open and close a register session, and create a clinical record with attachments.

The price difference is not a quality signal in the direction vendors assume: the features that matter for day-to-day operations — real-time batch-level inventory, structured clinical records, session-based point of sale — are exactly the parts of legacy systems that cost extra as modules. All-inclusive pricing means the vendor's incentive is adoption, not upselling, which is why flat-fee platforms tend to ship more complete workflows rather than thinner ones.

## Hidden Costs, Annual Discounts, and Total Cost of Ownership

The advertised price is rarely the real cost: across the four systems above, hidden costs — implementation, migration, training, hardware, per-user fees, and data-export charges — typically add 20-50% to the first-year total. Annual discounts (usually 10-20% for prepayment) offset part of it, but only a full TCO view tells you what you will really spend.

Common hidden costs in veterinary software contracts:

1. **Implementation and setup fees** — billed upfront, sometimes equal to several months of subscription
2. **Data migration** — converting paper records or another system's data is usually charged per record set
3. **Training** — on-site or group training beyond the basic onboarding package costs extra
4. **Hardware and IT** — on-premise systems (AVImark) require servers and support; cloud systems require reliable internet
5. **Per-user or per-branch fees** — the bill scales with your team or locations, not your usage
6. **Integration fees** — lab, imaging, and payment integrations are often separate monthly line items
7. **Support tiers** — priority support beyond standard hours can double the support charge
8. **Annual price increases** — review the contract language before signing, not at renewal
9. **Data-export fees** — the cost of getting your data out when you leave; ask about it before you sign

The pattern is consistent across vendors: the more opaque the pricing, the more line items appear after the signature. A mid-size clinic quoted at $350/month routinely spends $4,000-$7,000 in year one beyond the subscription — which is why the questions at the end of this guide ask for a full first-year cost breakdown in writing, not just the monthly figure.

Annual discounts worth negotiating:

- **Prepay one year** — vendors typically discount 10-20% off the monthly rate for annual billing
- **Multi-year contracts** — a 2-3 year commitment usually unlocks a deeper discount, but ties you to the vendor
- **Early renewal** — if the current plan is expiring, ask about the new-customer offer before it lapses
- **Bundled modules** — ask for the all-inclusive price even when the vendor sells modules separately

| AVImark | $4,500 - $9,000 (incl. hardware) | $2,000 - $5,000/yr | $12,500 - $29,000 |
| Provet Cloud | $7,000 - $9,000 | $6,500 - $9,000/yr | $33,000 - $45,000 |
| VETEK | $400 - $1,200 | $300 - $1,000/yr | $1,600 - $5,200 |

The gap is not the monthly fee — it is the compounding effect of per-user pricing, module add-ons, and implementation charges. A clinic that grows from three to six veterinarians sees Provet Cloud's bill nearly double, while a flat-fee system stays put. Note what the ranges do not include: payment processing, hardware replacement, and the staff time spent administering the system. Add those and the spread between the top and bottom of the table widens further — which is exactly why the TCO question should be asked in five-year terms, not monthly terms.

### How to Budget Your First Year

Build the year-one budget before you negotiate, so every fee lands on the table at once. Start with the quoted subscription multiplied by twelve, add the implementation quote, add migration and training, add hardware you genuinely need (scanners and label printers for batch work, for example), and add one contingency line of 10% for the items nobody quoted. Then ask the vendor to confirm that list line by line in writing — vendors that refuse to confirm line items are telling you something. The clinic that budgets this way routinely finds its year-one total differs from the advertised monthly price by 50% or more, and discovers the difference before signing instead of after.

### Questions to Ask Before You Sign

1. **Is this price per clinic, per user, or per location?** The answer changes your 5-year cost more than anything else.
2. **What is included in the base price?** Get the complete module list in writing and check it against your workflows.
3. **What are the implementation and migration costs?** Get a written quote before signing.
4. **What discount is available for annual billing?** 10-20% is the typical range; negotiate before you commit.
5. **How are price increases handled at renewal?** Is there a cap, and is it written into the contract?
6. **What does data export cost if we leave?** The exit fee is the hidden cost nobody quotes.
7. **Are there per-branch fees for multi-location practices?** Many vendors charge per location on top of the base price.

Per-user and per-module models mean your software costs more every time your practice grows.
